For the rest of you less religious puzzlers here's some more 168 facts from: [the wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/168_(number))

- 168 is an even number, a [composite number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_number), an [abundant number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abundant_number), and an [idoneal number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idoneal_number).
- There are 168 [primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_number) less than [1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1000_(number)). 168 is the product of the first two [perfect numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_number).
- 168 is the order of the group [PSL(2,7)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSL(2,7)), the second smallest nonabelian [simple group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_group).
- From [Hurwitz's automorphisms theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurwitz%27s_automorphisms_theorem), 168 is the maximum possible number of automorphisms of a genus 3 [Riemann surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_surface), this maximum being achieved by the [Klein quartic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein_quartic), whose symmetry group is PSL(2,7).[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/168_(number)#cite_note-2) The [Fano plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fano_plane) has 168 symmetries.
- 168 is the sum of four consecutive [prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_number): [37](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37_(number)) + [41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/41_(number)) + [43](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/43_(number)) + [47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/47_(number)).

Next up we have a  fascinating meta blog post: [https://www.scottaaronson.com/blog/?p=4133](https://www.scottaaronson.com/blog/?p=4133)  by Scott Aaronson about the replacement of classical  human proofs by machine generated and other variants.  Despite their promise, this possible future direction has not overshadowed tradition yet.

"In 1993, the science writer [John Horgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Horgan_(journalist))—who’s best known for his book [The End of Science](https://www.amazon.com/End-Science-Knowledge-Twilight-Scientific/dp/0465065929), and (of course) for [interviewing me in 2016](https://blogs.scientificamerican.com/cross-check/scott-aaronson-answers-every-ridiculously-big-question-i-throw-at-him/)—wrote a now-(in)famous cover article for Scientific American entitled [“The Death of Proof.”](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/4e56/c6fea76922082400dfc6e13a3a169ae7b9a6.pdf) Mashing together a large number of (what I’d consider) basically separate trends and ideas, Horgan argued that math was undergoing a fundamental change, with traditional deductive proofs being replaced by a combination of non-rigorous numerical simulations, machine-generated proofs, probabilistic and probabilistically-checkable proofs, and proofs using graphics and video. Horgan also suggested that Andrew Wiles’s then-brand-new [proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiles%27s_proof_of_Fermat%27s_Last_Theorem) of Fermat’s Last Theorem—which might have looked, at first glance, like a spectacular counterexample to the “death of proof” thesis—could be the “last gasp of a dying culture” and a “splendid anachronism.” Apparently, “The Death of Proof” garnered one of the largest volumes of angry mail in Scientific American‘s history, with mathematician after mathematician arguing that Horgan had strung together half-digested quotes and vignettes to manufacture a non-story."

I'm fairly interested in the  golden ratio as a quick browse of this blog will easily confirm so I was delighted to read: [http://extremelearning.com.au/going-beyond-golden-ratio/](http://extremelearning.com.au/going-beyond-golden-ratio/) from Martin Roberts.  Roberts takes on one of the best written explanations of the subtleties of the ratio from continued fractions to mobeius transforms and all in a really engaging framing.

"Let us imagine a game between two kids, Emily and Sam – both strong and determined in their own way who spend their entire lives trying to outwit each other, instead of doing their homework. (A real life Generative Adversial Network…)

Emily, proudly reminds us that she simultaneously bears the same first name as [Emily Davison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Davison), the most famous of British suffragettes; [Emily Balch](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1946/balch/biographical/), Nobel Peace Prize laureate; [Emilie du Chatelet](https://www.aps.org/publications/apsnews/200812/physicshistory.cfm), who wrote the first French translation and commentary of Isaac Newton’s “Principia“; [Emily Roebling](https://pursuit.unimelb.edu.au/articles/the-women-rebels-geniuses-and-pioneers-who-inspired-us), Chief Engineer of New York’s iconic Brooklyn Bridge; [Emily Bronte](https://theconversation.com/why-emily-brontes-wuthering-heights-is-a-cult-classic-100748) author of Wuthering Heights; [Emily Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Davison), the first female editor of ‘New Scientist‘ publication; and also [Emmy Noether](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/emmy-noether-theorem-legacy-physics-math), who revolutionized the field of theoretical physics.

On the other side we have Sam (and all his minion friends, who are aptly called Sam-002, Sam-003, Sam-004 ) who is part human / part robot and plays Minecraft or watches Youtube, 24/7.

They agree to play a game where Emily thinks of a number, and then Sam (with the possible help of his minions) has 60 seconds to find any fractions that are equal to Emily’s number.

And so the game begins…"
