As is traditional here are some facts about 181 courtesy of the Wikipedia: 

181 is a [centered number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centered_polygonal_number)
181 is a [centered pentagonal number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centered_pentagonal_number)
181 is a [centered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centered_polygonal_number) 12-gonal number
181 is a [centered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centered_polygonal_number) 18-gonal number[[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/181_(number)#cite_note-3)
181 is a [centered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centered_polygonal_number) 30-gonal number
181 is a [centered square number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centered_square_number)
181 is a [star number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_number) that represents a centered [hexagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagram) (as in the game of [Chinese checkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_checkers))

181 is a [deficient number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deficient_number), as 1 is less than 181
181 is an odious number
181 is a [prime number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_number)
181 is a [Chen prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_prime)
181 is a [dihedral prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_prime)
181 is a [full reptend prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_reptend_prime)
181 is a [palindromic prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindromic_prime)
181 is a strobogrammatic prime, the same when viewed upside down
181 is a [twin prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_prime) with [179](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/179_(number))

181 is a [square-free number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square-free_integer)
181 is an [undulating number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undulating_number), if written in the [ternary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternary_numeral_system), the [negaternary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_base), or the nonary [numeral systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numeral_system)
181 is the difference of 2 [square numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_number): 912 – 902
181 is the sum of 2 [consecutive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consecutive) [square numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_number): 92 + 102
181 is the sum of 5 [consecutive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consecutive) [prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_number): [29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/29_(number)) + [31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31_(number)) + [37](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37_(number)) + [41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/41_(number)) + [43](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/43_(number))

Next up two posts in honor of the late John Conway

Gianluigi Filippelli writes a lovely remembrance at  http://mathematics-in-europe.eu/?p=1713

"The most famous mathematical invention by John Conway was undoubtedly the Game of life, that was popolirized by Martin Gardner on his column published on Scientific American [1], but today I want to tell you something about the free will theorem.

The theorem was proposed by Conway with Simon Kochen, inspired by the question about the interpretation of quantum mechanics. The statement is:

If the choice of directions in which to perform spin 1 experiments is not a function of the information accessible to the experimenters, then the responsesof the particles are equally not functions of the information accessible to them."
